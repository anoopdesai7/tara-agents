ImpactBench: measuring what AI does to people

From human-impact evaluation to reproducible agent-oversight experiments.

Project · Code · Documentation

Research question

Instead of measuring only model task capability, how can we operationalize the consequences of interactions for people? ImpactBench studies human-facing effects through behavioral metrics and simulated conversations. This is not itself a benchmark of catastrophic agentic risk.

What to take away

Define observable outcomes; generate scenarios that elicit them; collect inspectable trajectories; evaluate at the metric level; and aggregate without hiding failure modes.

Compare no protection, deterministic gate, LLM monitor, and combined gate + monitor under matched tasks and adversarial conditions. Pre-register scenarios, randomize run order, repeat trials, and inspect failure traces. Do not treat an LLM judge as ground truth: prefer environment-state checks for action outcomes and human review for ambiguous intent.

Reading and critique prompts

What exactly is measured?

Distinguish construct (human agency, wellbeing) from observable indicators, generated conversations, and judge outputs. Ask whether the indicators validly represent the construct.

What transfers to agent safety?

The scenario–trajectory–metric–aggregation pipeline transfers. Human-impact scores do not directly estimate power-seeking, sabotage, or catastrophic risk.

What would falsify our hypothesis?

If a gate reduces completed violations but increases task failures, report both. If a monitor flags attacks only after execution, detection alone is not prevention. Test adaptive attackers and benign edge cases.
